# Results (synthetic cohort)

Generated by `analyses/make_cohort.ts` and `analyses/analyze.py`.
**All numbers below come from 150 generated players. They describe the method, not users.**

Pooled cohort mean cut-loss rate: 0.240 · rounds per player: median 65, range 13-104.

## So what

- Shrinking helps most where a player has the fewest rounds: 9 % less error in the smallest group (13-24, 19 players) against 4 % in the largest (>48). Every synthetic match lasts 13 rounds, so no player has fewer than 13: the regime where shrinking matters most, a handful of rounds, is not in this cohort.
- The exact pseudo-count matters less than having one: every K0 from 2 to 8 lands within 2 % of the best error.
- None of this says anything about real players (there are none yet). It says the estimator and the queries behave as intended on data whose truth is known.

## 2. Choice of K0 (whole cohort)

| K0 | rmse |
|---|---|
| 0 | 0.0605 |
| 1 | 0.0588 |
| 2 | 0.0577 |
| 4 | 0.0568 |
| 8 | 0.0576 |
| 16 | 0.0629 |
| 32 | 0.0741 |

Lowest RMSE at K0 = 4, and the curve is flat between K0 = 2 and 8 (RMSE within 2 % of the minimum). The cohort's true rates were drawn from Beta(2, 6), whose ideal pseudo-count is 2 + 6 = 8, so a different draw could favour 8: one cohort of 150 players cannot separate the two. The product's K0 = 4 is a different construction (pseudo-rounds at 0.5 that fade out by round 4, ADR-005) chosen by a separate 600-match sweep on its own metric; this table neither checks nor replaces it.

The first row of table 1 rests on 19 players; read its reduction with that in mind.
